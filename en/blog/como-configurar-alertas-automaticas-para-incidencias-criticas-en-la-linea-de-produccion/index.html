Automatic alerts for critical incidents

By Claudia · December 4, 2025 · 9 min read

In a modern plant, where every minute of downtime can mean significant losses, reacting quickly to critical incidents is an absolute priority. Unplanned stoppages, serious quality defects or failures in essential equipment can have a direct impact on productivity, costs and customer satisfaction. In this context, automatic alerts in incident management have become an indispensable tool for ensuring an immediate, coordinated response.

The automation of alerts for critical incidents on the production line not only allows action in real time, but also improves communication between teams, reduces the risk of human error and strengthens the traceability of every event.

In this article we'll look at what automatic alerts are, what their benefits are, how to set them up correctly, what mistakes to avoid and how tools like Solved make it possible to implement them simply, effectively and fully integrated with production systems.

What automatic alerts are in incident management

The automatic alerts for critical incidents on the production line are notifications generated by a digital system when an event occurs that requires immediate attention. These alerts are triggered in real time and sent to the designated owners through different channels (email, mobile notification or visual panel).

Instead of waiting for someone to manually report the problem or review a report, the system detects the incident, classifies it by severity and automatically sends an alert to the right people, indicating the type of failure, its location and the time available to act.

For example:

  • If an essential machine stops unexpectedly, the system sends a critical alert to the maintenance manager and the shift supervisor.
  • If a quality problem is detected in a packaged batch, the quality control manager and the line operator are notified.
  • If a safety risk occurs, the system alerts the prevention department and activates an emergency protocol.

These automatic alerts for critical incidents ensure that information reaches the right people at the exact moment, minimizing reaction times.

Benefits of having automatic alerts

Implementing a system of automatic alerts for critical incidents on the production line brings tangible advantages in the efficiency, safety and quality of industrial processes.

1. Faster, more coordinated response
 The main advantage of automatic alerts is the reduction in reaction time. Instead of relying on manual calls or emails, the system immediately notifies all the owners involved. This makes it possible to act within seconds, not minutes or hours.

2. Prevention of prolonged stoppages
 A critical incident that isn't addressed in time can stop an entire production line. With automatic alerts, maintenance or quality teams can step in before the problem gets worse, avoiding losses in productivity and materials.

3. Greater operational safety
 In situations where staff safety is at stake (leaks, fires, electrical failures, contamination), automatic alerts are essential. They make it possible to activate emergency protocols, notify staff on the plant floor and reduce the risk of accidents.

4. Regulatory compliance and traceability
 ISO standards, IFS o BRCGS require demonstrating that the company has control and response mechanisms in place for incidents. A system with automatic alerts for critical incidents keeps a detailed record of each alert, its recipient, and response times.

5. Reduction of human error
 Automated systems eliminate the possibility of an incident being forgotten or reported to the wrong person. Each alert is generated according to predefined rules, ensuring accuracy and reliability.

6. Continuous improvement based on data
 By recording each alert and its response, the system makes it possible to analyze failure patterns and measure team performance during critical incidents. This facilitates continuous improvement and the optimization of industrial processes.

How to configure effective automatic alerts

For automatic alerts for critical incidents to be truly useful, activating them is not enough: they must be configured strategically, adapted to the plant's workflow and each user's responsibilities. Below are the essential steps for achieving an effective setup.

1. Define criticality criteria

The first step is to establish what is considered a critical incident. Not all alerts should have the same priority. Correctly classifying the severity of events prevents overwhelming the team with unnecessary notifications.

Some common criticality criteria include:

  • Total or partial stoppage of the production line.
  • Failure of an essential machine or a key sensor.
  • Failure to meet quality parameters (temperature, weight, sealing, etc.).
  • Safety or contamination risks.
  • Incidents that directly affect the customer or the final product.

Defining these levels (for example, critical, high, medium, low) allows the system to assign priorities and notify the appropriate owners according to the type of problem.

2. Determine owners and alert roles

Each type of incident must have an assigned set of owners: operators, supervisors, technicians, or managers. The system for automatic alerts on the production line must know who should receive each notification, avoiding redundancies or gaps in information.

Example structure:

  • Critical maintenance incidents: maintenance manager, on-shift technician, and plant owner.
  • Quality incidents: quality manager and line supervisor.
  • Safety incidents: prevention department and human resources.

By linking alerts to specific roles, each incident is guaranteed to reach the person with the actual capacity to resolve it.

3. Choose the appropriate alert channels

Not all alerts should be communicated the same way. It's important to select the channels that ensure an immediate response depending on the nature of the event.

The most common channels include:

  • Mobile notifications: ideal for operators and technicians on the plant floor.
  • Emails: useful for managers who are not physically on site at the plant.
  • Visual or audible alerts: panels in the production area that display active incidents.
  • Integrations with internal messaging systems: such as Microsoft Teams or Slack, for instant communication.

Combining channels increases the effectiveness of automatic alerts for critical incidents, ensuring the message gets through regardless of the user's device or location.

4. Define maximum response times

Each type of incident should have a time limit for resolution or initial response. If that time is exceeded, the system should escalate the alert to a higher level.

For example:

  • Critical incident → maximum response time: 5 minutes.
  • High-priority incident → 15 minutes.
  • Medium-priority incident → 30 minutes.

This time hierarchy keeps incidents from piling up or going unnoticed. It also generates valuable metrics on team performance and the efficiency of incident management on the production line.

5. Establish associated action protocols

An effective alert should not just notify, it should also indicate what to do. Linking an action protocol to each type of incident lets the team respond in a standardized way, without hesitation.

Example:

"Critical incident: temperature out of range on line 3. Immediate action: stop the machine, check the sensor, notify the maintenance manager."

These protocols can be built directly into the alert system so each user receives clear instructions along with the notification.

Common challenges and how to overcome them

Implementing automatic alerts for critical incidents can face certain initial challenges. However, with proper planning and the support of specialized software, they can be overcome easily.

1. Alert overload (alert fatigue)
 If the system generates too many notifications, users may start ignoring them. The solution is to set priority criteria and limit alerts to truly critical events.

2. Configuration errors
 Poorly configured alerts (for example, with the wrong recipients or no defined criticality parameters) can cause confusion. That's why it's essential to review the initial setup and test different scenarios before launching the system in production.

3. Team resistance
 At first, some operators or supervisors may see automatic alerts as an extra burden. To overcome this, it's key to training staff and show how alerts reduce stress, prevent errors, and make daily work easier.

4. Lack of integration with other systems
 If alerts aren't connected to ERP, MES, or CMMS, global visibility is lost. Integration between systems ensures data consistency and eliminates duplicate records.
