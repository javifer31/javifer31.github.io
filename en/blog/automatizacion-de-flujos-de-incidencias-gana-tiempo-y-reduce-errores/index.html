Incident workflow automation

By Claudia · November 29, 2025 · 9 min read

The incident management automation has become one of the most effective strategies for increasing efficiency in modern plants. In industrial environments where errors cost money and delays can bring entire production lines to a halt, automating incident logging, assignment and resolution processes is key to ensuring quality, traceability and speed.

Every minute counts. When incidents are assigned manually, teams lose time, human errors occur and tracking becomes harder. The incident management automation makes it possible for every problem to be routed automatically to the right owner, with real-time alerts and full traceability of every action.

In this article we'll explore what it means to automate incident workflows, what their benefits are, how they're configured, what challenges their implementation can present and how tools like Solved make it possible in a simple and effective way.

What incident workflow automation is

The incident management automation consists of designing a digital system that automatically assigns, notifies and monitors every incident from logging to closure. There's no longer any need for an owner to manually review reports or distribute tasks: the system analyzes the incident type, identifies its criticality level and sends it directly to the corresponding technician or area.

In practical terms, incident workflow automation is based on three fundamental components:

1. Triggers

These are the conditions that trigger an automatic action. For example: when a critical incident is logged, the system sends an immediate alert to the supervisor and opens a priority task.

2. Roles and owners

Every incident is linked to a set of defined roles: operator, supervisor, maintenance technician, quality manager, etc. The incident management automation relies on this structure to assign each case logically and quickly.

3. Alerts and notifications

Once the workflows are configured, the system sends automatic notifications by email or mobile app. These alerts keep users informed without the need for manual follow-up.

Thanks to these elements, plants can turn reactive management into a preventive, standardized and much more agile process.

Main advantages of incident workflow automation

The incident management automation delivers tangible benefits from day one. Its advantages show up in time saved as well as in work quality and information traceability.

Fewer human errors

Manual assignment often causes confusion, incidents with no owner or duplicated tasks. With the incident workflow automation, the system applies predefined rules and eliminates the possibility of error.

Faster assignment

A logged incident is sent immediately to the right technician or owner. This shortens reaction times and improves operational continuity. In fact, companies that apply incident management automation manage to cut response times by up to 60%.

Better traceability and control

Every incident generates a digital record: who reported it, when it was assigned, what actions were taken and when it was resolved. This ensures full transparency and makes audits, reports and real-time tracking easier.

Smooth communication between departments

Thanks to incident management automation, maintenance, quality and production departments work with the same information and avoid misunderstandings or overlapping tasks.

Simpler regulatory compliance

Standards like ISO 9001, ISO 22000 or BRCGS require traceability and control over incidents. With the incident workflow automation, documentation is generated automatically and stays ready for audits.

Examples of automated workflows

An automated workflow can be as simple or as complex as the operation requires. Below are some practical examples of incident management automation in plants.

1. Critical incident in production

When a fault is detected on a critical line, the operator logs it from a tablet. The system classifies the incident as “critical” and automatically assigns it to the shift supervisor and the specialized maintenance technician. Both receive an immediate notification. If the incident isn't resolved within a set timeframe, an escalated alert is generated to the plant manager.

2. Minor or preventive incident

If an operator detects a minor anomaly (for example, unusual vibration), the system tags it as “minor” and assigns it directly to the shift's maintenance operator. If it isn't closed before the end of the shift, the system transfers the task to the next owner.

3. Quality incident on the packaging line

When a defective label is detected, the operator reports it. The automated workflow notifies the quality manager, who starts the investigation. Once the root cause is determined, the system automatically creates a corrective action and assigns owners.

4. Repetitive incidents

The incident management automation also makes it possible to detect patterns. If the same type of fault repeats three times in a week, the system generates an alert for structural review or preventive maintenance.

These examples show how the incident workflow automation doesn't just speed up responses, it turns data into a tool for continuous improvement.

Challenges of automation and how to overcome them

Although the benefits are clear, implementing the incident management automation can present some initial challenges. With proper planning and support, all of them can be easily overcome.

Setting clear, realistic rules

One of the most common mistakes is creating overly complex workflows. It's best to start with simple rules: incident type, priority level and responsible area. As the team gets familiar with the system, additional levels can be added.

Avoiding notification overload

Too many alerts can cause overload and lose effectiveness. In the incident workflow automation, notifications should be configured based on criticality, not on every small change.

Training the teams

Automation doesn't replace staff, it frees them from repetitive tasks. It's essential to train users so they understand the workflow, know how to interpret alerts and correctly update the status of every incident.

Integrating with existing systems

To maximize the value of the incident management automation, it's important to connect the system with ERP, MES or CMMS. This avoids data duplication and ensures consistency across the whole operational chain.

Measuring results and continuously improving

Once the incident workflow automation, it's advisable to set performance indicators: average resolution time, number of incidents closed on time, recurrence rate, etc. This data helps fine-tune the workflows and keep them efficient.
