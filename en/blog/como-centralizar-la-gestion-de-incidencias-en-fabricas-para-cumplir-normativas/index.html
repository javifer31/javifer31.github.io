Centralizing incident management in plants

By Claudia · November 28, 2025 · 9 min read

In industrial plants, incidents happen daily: a production line stops, a batch shows a deviation, a machine requires urgent maintenance, or a quality non-conformity is detected.

In many plants, information is scattered across spreadsheets, emails, paper forms and different systems (ERP, MES, CMMS). This causes incomplete records, duplication, delays and one critical consequence: difficulty meeting quality and safety regulations.

Meeting standards such as ISO 9001, IFS, BRCGS or ISO 22000 requires full traceability, clear evidence and demonstrable response times. But when each department records incidents its own way, achieving that traceability becomes a monumental task.

In this article we'll look at how to centralize incident management in a single system, the benefits of doing so, the main challenges and how a platform like Solved can simplify the whole process so that meeting regulations becomes a natural part of daily work.

Problems with decentralized incident management

Lack of centralization poses multiple challenges for companies:

1. Duplicate data and loss of information

When incidents are managed across different systems (ERP, MES, CMMS or even emails and spreadsheets), data duplication is inevitable. The same incident can appear three times with a different description, owner or status.
This creates confusion, errors in reports and, in the worst case, decisions based on incorrect information.

2. Lack of traceability

Quality standards require being able to answer a simple question:

“What happened, who recorded it, what action was taken and when was it resolved?”

In decentralized environments, this chain of traceability breaks down constantly. There's no single history, and auditors often find gaps in the documentation.

Result: non-conformities, observations or penalties in audits.

3. Delays in resolving incidents

When owners have to search for information across different channels, response times skyrocket.
Without a centralized system that automatically assigns tasks and notifies the right owner, an incident can take days to resolve, affecting production or meeting due dates.

4. Direct impact on audits

Every audit becomes a race against the clock: gathering data, reviewing spreadsheets, searching emails, validating evidence…
Decentralization multiplies the time spent on audits and increases the risk of errors or omissions. What should be a quick check turns into a week of stress and duplicated work.

What centralizing incident management means

Centralizing incident management means concentrating all relevant information —record, tracking, evidence, owners and results— in a a single shared, accessible system for all departments involved: production, maintenance, quality, safety, environment, etc.

The key components of centralized management

  1. Single incident record
     Each incident is documented once and classified by type, area and priority.
  2. Defined owners and workflows
     The system automatically assigns tasks and owners, avoiding confusion or duplication.
  3. Digital evidence and full traceability
     Photos, videos, documents and comments are linked to the case, creating a verifiable history.
  4. Integration with other systems (ERP, MES, CMMS)
     Information flows between platforms without the need to copy data manually.
  5. Automatic alerts and notifications
     Each user receives real-time alerts about relevant incidents, due dates or deviations.
  6. Real-time reports
     Owners can view the overall status from a dashboard, without waiting for monthly reports.

In short: a single source of truth for all incidents.

Benefits of centralizing incident management

1. Greater traceability and control

Each incident has a complete history from detection to closure.
This makes it easier to comply with standards such as ISO 9001, which require evidence of traceability in the corrective actions and preventive (CAPA).
In addition, having all the data centralized makes it possible to analyze trends and detect failure patterns.

2. Fewer errors and duplications

With a single system, incidents are only recorded once.
This eliminates duplicates and ensures consistency across departments.
Data no longer depends on each employee's memory or emails.

3. Time savings in audits

With all the information digitized and traceable, preparing for an audit stops being a burden.
Auditors can access records, evidence and actions directly, reducing review time by more than 70 %.
In addition, teams gain confidence knowing that everything is documented correctly.

4. Simpler regulatory compliance

The main quality and industrial safety standards require documented control of incidents, response times and corrective actions.
Centralizing records makes it easier to comply with:

  • ISO 9001 – Quality management system
  • ISO 22000 – Food safety
  • IFS / BRCGS – Food safety and traceability
  • ISO 45001 – Occupational health and safety
  • ISO 14001 – Environment

With a centralized tool, evidence is generated automatically and kept without risk of loss or tampering.

5. Better communication between departments

Centralizing also improves collaboration. Production, maintenance and quality work on the same information in real time.

Silos are eliminated and transparency is gained, which speeds up decision-making and strengthens the continuous improvement culture.

Challenges of centralizing management (and how to overcome them)

1. Team resistance to change

Some employees may be reluctant to abandon Excel or their traditional forms.

The key lies in showing tangible benefits from day one: less repetitive work, more visibility and fewer errors.

Tip: start with a pilot in a small area and show measurable results (for example, a 30% reduction in average resolution time).

2. Migrating historical data

Moving information from spreadsheets or legacy systems may seem like an obstacle, but with the right methodology it's entirely feasible

The ideal approach is to define which data actually adds value, clean up duplicates and migrate only what's needed.

3. Integration with existing systems (ERP, MES, CMMS)

A centralized system must communicate without friction with the plant's other systems.

Modern platforms use APIs or standard connectors, which allows data to be synced automatically:

  • Incidents detected on the plant floor are updated in the ERP.
  • Maintenance orders are also closed in the CMMS.
  • Dashboards reflect changes in real time.

This way, duplicate information is avoided and consistency is maintained across all environments.

4. Security and access control

Another critical aspect is ensuring that data is protected.

A centralized system must offer user profiles, change history, encryption y automatic backups, ensuring compliance with GDPR and the organization's internal policies.
