Quality report automation

By Pablo · November 12, 2025 · 6 min read

The quality report automation is one of the most powerful levers factories have today to gain efficiency, reduce errors and ensure the traceability of their processes. Traditionally, quality teams have spent hours — or even entire days — consolidating scattered information: photos taken on the plant floor, documents saved on personal computers, paper records and data sent via WhatsApp or email. This system not only consumes time, but also increases the chances of making critical errors in audits or when reporting incidents to suppliers and customers.

The future of quality lies in digitizing and automating data collection, analysis and presentation. This article explores why quality report automation is essential in modern industry, what its benefits are, what steps must be followed to implement it and how specialized tools like Solved are transforming the way teams manage information.

The problem with manual quality reports

Before talking about quality report automation, it's necessary to understand the challenges of manual processes. Many factories still rely on traditional methods that involve:

  • Going down to the plant floor to take a photo of the incident.
  • Sending the image via WhatsApp or email to the owner.
  • Downloading it to the computer and pasting it into a Word document.
  • Adding manual comments, copying data from an Excel spreadsheet and formatting the file.
  • Sending it by email to the supplier, the customer or management.

This process can be repeated dozens of times a day, creating bottlenecks. The result is an overloaded quality team, inconsistent reports and lost traceability. In audits, this method multiplies the risk of non-conformities because records aren't always complete or properly documented.

What is quality report automation

The quality report automation involves using a digital system that collects data the moment it's generated, organizes it automatically and creates standardized reports ready to be shared.

An automated system enables:

  • Recording incidents on the plant floor with photos and videos from a phone or tablet.
  • Storing information securely in the cloud.
  • Generating automatic reports with the necessary data.
  • Sharing reports with customers, suppliers or auditors in seconds.

The difference is huge: what used to take hours of manual consolidation is reduced to seconds with automation.

Benefits of quality report automation

Implementing quality report automation offers immediate, measurable advantages:

Time savings

Teams stop spending hours collecting and formatting information. This frees up resources for strategic analysis and continuous improvement tasks.

Fewer human errors

Manual processes are prone to failures: incomplete data, wrong copies, poorly structured reports. With automation, information flows directly from the record to the final report with no intermediate steps.

Standardization

Every report follows a defined format, which makes audits and communication with suppliers and customers easier.

Full traceability

Reports include digital evidence (photos, videos, attached documents) and a clear history of who recorded the incident, when, and what actions were taken.

Audit readiness

With quality report automation, a factory can demonstrate compliance in ISO 9001, ISO 22000, IFS or BRC audits without spending weeks preparing documents.

Competitive advantage

The ability to respond quickly and transparently to customers and auditors builds trust and strengthens the company's reputation.

Steps to implement quality report automation

Adopting quality report automation requires planning. These are the key steps:

1. Identify critical processes

Define what types of incidents, audits or checks must be recorded and reported systematically.

2. Standardize data collection

Create categories and templates to record incidents consistently across the organization.

3. Digitize evidence capture

Equip teams with mobile devices and apps that allow attaching photos, videos and documents in real time.

4. Set up automated workflows

Define how incidents are assigned, how reports are generated and who they're sent to automatically.

5. Integrate with other systems

Make sure reports are connected with ERP, MES or GMAO to avoid duplication and keep data consistent.

6. Train staff

Train teams on how to use the digital tools to ensure smooth adoption.

7. Measure results

Set KPIs to assess impact: report generation time, error reduction, and customer and auditor satisfaction.

Common challenges in automation

Although the benefits are clear, some factories face obstacles:

  • Resistance to change: some employees see digitization as red tape.
  • Connectivity issues on the plant floor that make synchronization difficult.
  • Fear of losing control: managers who believe manual systems are safer because "they control them".

The key is showing quick, tangible results. Once teams see how quality report automation saves them hours of work, adoption speeds up.

Real-world report automation examples

A real example is a canning factory that used to take up to 3 days to consolidate an incident report for a supplier. With automation, the report is generated automatically in under 15 minutes, digital evidence included.

Another case is a textile company that had to prepare internal audits weeks in advance. After implementing quality report automation, auditors can access the platform directly to verify records, cutting preparation time by 70%.

These examples show that automation isn't just a technology issue, but a cultural shift that directly impacts efficiency and competitiveness.
