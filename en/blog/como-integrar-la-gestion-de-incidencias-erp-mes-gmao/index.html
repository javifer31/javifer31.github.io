Integrate incidents with your ERP, MES or CMMS

By Karim · October 7, 2025 · 6 min read

The incident management with an ERP is one of the biggest challenges in plants working with multiple digital systems. 

In many cases, incidents or non-conformities arise in different departments and need to be managed cross-functionally: quality, production, maintenance, logistics and even purchasing or sales. 

The problem is that the tools already in place on the plant floor —ERP, MES or CMMS— aren't designed to record or give full traceability to those incidents. The result: duplications, slow processes, lack of visibility and a loss of time that directly impacts quality and costs.

In this article we'll look at why this problem happens, what role each system plays, what the benefits of integrating incident management into your digital ecosystem are, and how Solved provides a simpler, more efficient solution that avoids modifying existing software.

What duplicating data means and why it isn't the main problem

When we talk about duplicating data in ERP incident management, we mean having to record the same incident on different platforms: for example, noting a batch fault in the ERP, documenting it again in the CMMS so the maintenance team can review it, and perhaps in the MES so it's reflected in production. This double or triple entry creates inconsistencies, errors and unnecessary administrative burden.

But the real problem isn't just duplication: it's the lack of cross-departmental scope and traceability.

  • ERP is designed to manage inventory, purchasing, invoicing or logistics, but not to follow an incident from when it happens until it's resolved.
  • MES controls production in real time, but its focus is the production process, not overall quality or the corrective actions.
  • CMMS manages asset maintenance, but leaves out quality incidents, suppliers or audits.

This forces the creation of complex information flows that require software modifications to try to get them to communicate with each other. In practice, few plants achieve smooth integration and, meanwhile, lose visibility of what really matters: closing incidents fast and with full traceability.

Systems involved and their roles: ERP, MES and CMMS

To better understand where ERP incident management falls short, let's look at the role each system plays:

  • ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning): is the core of business management. It's used for finance, purchasing, inventory, orders and, in some cases, quality. However, it isn't designed to manage non-conformities with evidence, owner workflows or real-time tracking.
  • MES (Manufacturing Execution System): controls and monitors production on the plant floor. It shows line efficiency, work orders or machine status. Although it gathers valuable data, its scope is limited to the production environment and it doesn't easily integrate supplier, customer or audit incidents.
  • CMMS (Computerized Maintenance Management System): focused on preventive and corrective asset maintenance. It's useful for technical incidents, but leaves out quality issues, cross-departmental processes or documented non-conformities.

Ultimately, each system serves a specific function. The problem arises when an incident affects several departments and there's no common thread to follow it from start to finish.

Benefits of integrating incident management across ERP, MES and CMMS

Integrating ERP incident management delivers clear benefits:

  • Full traceability: from the moment an incident is detected until it's resolved, everything is documented, regardless of the area.
  • Elimination of duplicate records: avoids the need to note the same thing in several systems.
  • Faster response time: incidents go straight to the right owner, without losing information.
  • Greater visibility in audits: you have a single, verifiable history.
  • Operational optimization: less downtime, smoother processes and continuous learning to prevent recurrences.

However, integrating these systems is usually a challenge. It requires custom development, complex data flows and an IT investment that many plants aren't willing to make.

Common challenges when integrating systems

  1. Technological differences: each piece of software uses different formats, APIs and data structures.
  2. Change management: staff may resist new ways of recording or managing data.
  3. Data synchronization: real time or batch? Refresh time can be critical.
  4. Security and permissions: sharing data between systems demands rigorous management.
  5. Implementation costs: each integration is a project in itself, with a risk of cost overruns.

That's why many companies look for solutions that don't force them to "force" the integration of every system, but instead act as a cross-cutting layer.

Steps for effective integration (methodology)

  1. Internal audit of existing systems
     Map how incidents are recorded in ERP, MES and GMAO. Identify duplications and gaps.
  2. Define incident flows and data mapping
     Decide which data should be shared between systems (owner, incident type, status, evidence).
  3. Choose the integration interface or method
     APIs, standard connectors or middleware. Assess cost and complexity.
  4. Design a common data structure
     Standardize categories, priorities and owners to avoid confusion.
  5. Run pilot tests
     Start in one specific area before rolling out to the whole plant.
  6. Train staff and define clear roles
     Make sure everyone knows where and how to record.
  7. Monitor and adjust after rollout
     Measure duplicate incidents, resolution times, unattended alerts.
