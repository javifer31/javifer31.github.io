An incident that comes back is not a new incident

It is the same one, still unsolved, and closing it again fixes nothing. The root cause analysis — five whys or an Ishikawa diagram — is done inside the report itself and by the book, and the corrective action that comes out of it stays attached to its analysis instead of living in a document nobody opens again.

1

The symptom

Product rejected on line 3. It is what you see, what stops the line and what goes into the shift check. Closing here is just putting out the fire.

2

Why?

The pack seal was faulty. Still an effect: nobody has decided anything yet, we have only gone down one floor.

3

Why?

The sealer's gasket was worn. Now there is something physical, but swapping it and going back to work leaves the problem untouched.

4

The cause

There was no preventive replacement plan. This one can be acted on, and the corrective action that follows — setting up the preventive job in the CMMS — is saved attached to this analysis.

Frequently asked questions

How is an incident logged in Solved?

From a phone, tablet or PC, right at the workstation: describe the deviation, attach photos or video and assign an owner.

Can incident handling be automated?

Yes. The workflows are configurable, so each incident is routed to the right owner with no manual step.

Does Solved handle non-conformities?

Yes. Incidents and non-conformities are handled in the same system, tracked from the first alert to the corrective action and its closure.

Does it integrate with ERP, MES or CMMS?

Yes. Solved connects to the production and maintenance systems already in place, which avoids duplicated data.

Can I see incident metrics and reports?

Yes. The system gives you real-time charts and trends to spot hot spots and get ahead of repeat failures.
